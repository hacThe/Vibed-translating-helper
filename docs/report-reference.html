Patterns worth taking with you.

You don’t have to memorize every correction. These six habits will make the biggest difference in your next translation.

01

Protect the meaning first

Check who, what, when, and how much. “Not just a target” and “not a target” communicate different ideas. Passengers and flight attendants are different people.

không chỉ… mà…
→ not only… but also…
02

Learn phrases, not isolated words

Choose words for this topic. “Occlusion” can describe a blockage in a technical context, but “bottleneck” fits transport news. An “industrial park” contains multiple facilities.

đầu mối giao thông → transport hub
tháo gỡ ùn tắc → ease congestion
03

Match the subject and verb

“The city” is singular; “projects” is plural. After “one of,” use a plural noun because you are selecting one member of a group.

The city contribute → contributes
one of the top priority → priorities
04

Use the passive only when it fits

Someone builds a project, so a project can “be built.” Something simply happens or exists; these verbs do not take passive forms in these sentences.

Projects are being built.
Hotspots have existed for years.
Similar problems also occurred.
05

Keep lists grammatically balanced

After “will,” keep each coordinated verb in its base form. The same principle applies to “not only… but also…” constructions.

The projects will ease congestion,
improve connectivity, and
support economic development.
06

Check dates, units, and word forms

“By 2030” gives a deadline. “Over five years” describes a period. “Roll out” is a verb; “rollout” is a noun. Use singular “billion” after a number.

500.000 tỷ đồng → VND 500 trillion
255k metro → 255 km of metro lines
quốc lộ 13 → National Highway 13

Your three-pass editing routine

01 / Check the meaningVerify names, titles, numbers, units, and words such as “not,” “only,” and “also.”
02 / Check the grammarFind each subject and its verb. Check agreement, tense, and active or passive voice.
03 / Read for natural EnglishPut the Vietnamese aside. Read aloud and replace awkward wording with familiar phrases.
